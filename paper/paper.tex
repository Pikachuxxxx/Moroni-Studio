\title{MORONI: Polygon-Guided Cellular Textures via Roots-of-Unity Polygons and Phase-Modulated Boundaries}

\author{Phani Srikar}
\affiliation{\institution{Independent Researcher}\city{}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
Voronoi and Worley noise are foundational cellular primitives in graphics,
but nearest-site assignment does not independently prescribe arbitrary target
polygon silhouettes and their contested boundaries.

This paper presents \textbf{MORONI}, a cellular texture technique that
parameterizes cells as jittered roots-of-unity polygons and assigns pixels via
a three-term energy combining center distance, signed polygon distance, and
angular phase modulation. An optional coverage margin clips assigned regions
to release background space. Across eight seeds, matched polygon clipping
raises triangular target IoU from $0.551$ to $0.939$ for Voronoi and from
$0.556$ to $0.945$ for MORONI at the default phase weight. Disabling phase raises
MORONI's clipped IoU to $0.954$. Clipping supplies most silhouette fidelity;
phase provides boundary variation and can reduce fidelity. Coverage is
monotonic in the margin, without a constant-width mortar guarantee.

An enclosing-disk lower bound and adaptive finite-lattice search accelerate
CPU evaluation. Accelerated and full searches agree on 737,280 tested pixels
across 120 analytical/hybrid cases. Hybrid distance uses bilinear lookups
inside each lookup box and analytical polygon distance outside. At $72\times72$,
the measured mean absolute distance error is $2.15\times10^{-5}$ over the
specified query distribution; default-field label disagreement is $0.00625\%$.
A WebGL2 companion studio shares generated geometry with the CPU code and
exports unlit albedo, normal, height, and roughness maps. GPU timings use
disjoint timer queries on the recorded device. We distinguish mathematical
bounds, finite numerical tests, and descriptive raster geometry throughout.
The studio and code are available at
\url{https://pikachuxxxx.github.io/Moroni-Studio/} and
\url{https://github.com/Pikachuxxxx/Moroni-Studio}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cellular noise is foundational to procedural visual effects, game rendering,
and virtual worlds. Natural and architectural surfaces --- cracked desert mud,
masonry stone walls, ceramic floor tiles, shattered glass, reptilian scales, and
weathered paint --- exhibit characteristic cellular tiling structures. In modern
real-time pipelines (Unreal Engine Material Graph, Unity Shader Graph, Blender
Cycles/EEVEE, and Substance 3D Designer), Voronoi and Worley
noise~\cite{voronoi1908,worley1996} are widely used for their compact
representations and efficient evaluation.

Despite their ubiquity, point-site cellular primitives couple site placement and cell boundaries.
For classical Euclidean Voronoi diagrams, boundaries lie on perpendicular
bisectors. A square lattice can produce square cells; prescribed arbitrary
targets cannot generally be controlled independently while retaining a full
partition. Domain warping provides a useful alternative but changes the
coordinate frame and hence the targets as well as their boundaries.

Weighted diagram generalizations relax the perpendicular bisector constraint,
but remain structurally constrained. Power (Laguerre)
diagrams~\cite{aurenhammer1987power} replace Euclidean distance
$\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}_c\rVert$ with $\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}_c\rVert^2 - w_c$,
and additively weighted (Apollonius) diagrams replace it with
$\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}_c\rVert - w_c$. However, power diagram cells are
intersections of affine half-planes and are mathematically guaranteed to be
\emph{convex}; Apollonius cells are guaranteed to be \emph{connected}. Concavity is excluded by power diagrams, while nonempty additively weighted
cells are connected. Raster measurements alone do not prove that a continuous
MORONI cell violates either property.

\paragraph{Two Geometric Challenges in Polygon-Guided Cellular Synthesis.}
Bridging the gap between procedural efficiency and art directability requires
attaching explicit target polygons to cells. However, constructing an energy
field over polygon cells introduces two distinct geometric challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Boundary Distance Dilemma}: An unsigned distance
$\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial P_c)$ achieves its minimum \emph{on}
the boundary outline $\partial P_c$. Consequently, penalizing boundary distance
favors the perimeter over deeper interior points. A signed field favors interior points over the perimeter
when this term is minimized (\S\ref{sec:signed}). This preference does not
guarantee recovery of a target silhouette in a competing field.
\item \textbf{Space-Filling Constraint vs.\ Target Silhouettes}: Under a strict
space-filling $\arg\min$ partition, every pixel must be assigned to some cell,
leaving no unassigned space. For jittered sites, adjacent cells compete along
their common borders, distorting target shapes (such as triangles or quads)
into irregular polygons with compromise edges. Recovering clean target
silhouettes requires decoupling cell assignment from strict space filling.
Introducing a coverage margin $m$ releases pixels whose distance exceeds a
threshold, allowing cells to preserve their prescribed silhouettes while
simultaneously providing a monotonic coverage dial (\S\ref{sec:margin}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Summary of Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A polygon-guided cellular texture recipe combining center distance,
signed polygon distance, and angular phase modulation (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\item Matched clipping and phase ablations over eight seeds, exposing both
the modest assignment benefit and the fidelity cost of phase
(\S\ref{sec:control}).
\item Raster solidity and component measurements with explicit sampling,
connectivity and filtering conventions (\S\ref{sec:reach}).
\item Conservative candidate bounds, finite adaptive search, and a lookup
error analysis distinguishing approximation from acceleration
(\S\ref{sec:fast}).
\item A WebGL2 studio using shared geometry, actual GPU timer queries,
cross-implementation tests and explicitly defined map exports
(\S\ref{sec:app}).
\item Full generator algorithms and a comparative operational matrix
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_generators}).
\end{itemize}
These contributions combine established primitives; roots of unity and signed
distance are not claimed as new, and no new classification of weighted
Voronoi diagrams is asserted.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Procedural pattern synthesis in computer graphics spans gradient noise, cellular
diagrams, distance fields, and physical simulation.

\subsection{Procedural Noise Functions}
Procedural noise began with Perlin's gradient noise~\cite{perlin1985}, followed
by multi-scale spectral representations such as wavelet noise by Cook and
DeRose~\cite{cook2005wavelet}. Lagae et al.~\cite{lagae2009gabor} introduced Gabor
noise, enabling exact spectral control via sparse convolution. The comprehensive
survey by Lagae et al.~\cite{lagae2010noise} reviews noise functions for procedural texturing; modern rendering systems combine such functions with material models~\cite{pbr4}. While gradient
and Gabor noise excel at continuous, band-limited phenomena (clouds, marble, wood
grain), piecewise cellular assignment is a different primitive; thresholds and
domain warps can combine these families.

\subsection{Cellular Diagrams and Weighted Extensions}
Cellular noise descends from Georgy Voronoi's 1908 treatise on quadratic
forms~\cite{voronoi1908}. Steven Fortune~\cite{fortune1987} pioneered optimal
sweepline geometry for planar Voronoi diagrams. Lloyd~\cite{lloyd1982} introduced
iterative centroidal Voronoi relaxation, widely adopted in graphics for stippling
and remeshing. Steven Worley~\cite{worley1996} revolutionized shader texturing by
introducing the cellular basis function $F_k(\mathbf{x})$, measuring distances
to the $k$-th nearest feature points on a spatial grid. McEwan et al.~\cite{mcewan2012noise} discuss efficient computational noise
in GLSL; their work is not evidence for a particular MORONI implementation.

To provide size control, weighted Voronoi diagrams were introduced. In Power
diagrams (Laguerre geometry)~\cite{aurenhammer1987power}, each site carries a
scalar weight $w_c$ subtracting from squared distance:
$\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}_c\rVert^2 - w_c$. Power diagrams retain affine
linear bisectors and have convex cells. In additively weighted (Apollonius)
diagrams, weights subtract directly from Euclidean distance:
$\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}_c\rVert - w_c$, producing hyperbolic curved
boundaries. Nonempty additively weighted cells are connected~\cite{cgal_apollonius},
but may be nonconvex. Section~\ref{sec:reach} separates these continuous
properties from thresholded raster observations.

\subsection{Procedural Fracture vs.\ Physical Simulation}
Physical fracture modeling accurately simulates crack propagation using finite
element methods (FEM) or boundary element methods (BEM). O'Brien and
Hodgins~\cite{obrien1999fracture} established continuum mechanics fracture for
graphics. Desbenoit et al.~\cite{desbenoit2005cracks} modeled surface cracks and
peeling patterns, and Nealen et al.~\cite{nealen2006survey} surveyed physically
based deformable models. These methods address physical deformation and fracture. Their costs depend
on the model and discretization; we do not provide a timing comparison with
them or claim equivalent physical accuracy.

Procedural crack generation offers a lightweight alternative. Mould~\cite{mould2005fracture}
synthesized image-guided surface crack patterns using shortest-path graph propagation
on lattices. Such graph constructions offer a different control model. MORONI evaluates
a finite field independently per pixel, using uploaded polygon data; its
phase term supplies crack-like stylization, not physical fracture propagation.

\subsection{Implicit Surfaces and Distance Fields}
Distance transforms map discrete masks to continuous distance
fields~\cite{felzenszwalb2004dt}. Hart~\cite{hart1996spheretracing} demonstrated
sphere tracing against implicit signed distance fields (SDF). In real-time
demoscene and shader art, signed distance functions enable compact procedural
modeling. MORONI bridges signed distance fields with cellular partition theory,
using signed boundary fields to influence assignment, followed by optional
clipping. Neither term alone guarantees a target silhouette.

\subsection{Controllability in Procedural Content Generation}
Controllability is the central challenge in procedural content generation (PCG).
Smelik et al.~\cite{smelik2014survey} and Vanegas et
al.~\cite{vanegas2010modelling} emphasize that procedural algorithms must provide
intuitive, declarative handles rather than opaque mathematical parameters.
M{\"u}ller et al.~\cite{muller2006buildings} demonstrated rule-based grammar
derivation for procedural architecture, providing an example of structured procedural control. MORONI exposes
shape, size, irregularity, coverage, and phase controls, but these interact
and their usability has not been evaluated in an artist study.

Guehl et al.~\cite{guehl2020pptbf} combine point processes and rich kernels
in Point Process Texture Basis Functions. MORONI addresses the narrower case
of explicit finite polygon targets. No superiority in coverage, image matching,
or perceptual quality over PPTBF is claimed.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Cell Parameterization}

Each cell $c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$ owns a center $\mathbf{u}_c$ and an explicit
$n_c$-gon $P_c$ whose vertices are generated as jittered roots of unity:
\begin{equation}
z_{c,k} = r_c\exp\!\Bigl(i\bigl(\tfrac{2\pi k}{n_c}+\delta_{c,k}\bigr)\Bigr),
\quad \delta_{c,k}\sim\mathcal{U}(-J_c,J_c),
\label{eq:poly}
\end{equation}
for $k = 0,\dots,n_c-1$, offset by cell center $\mathbf{u}_c$. Centers are
positioned on a jittered Cartesian lattice of spacing $s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u}_c = \begin{pmatrix} c_x \cdot s + \xi_{c,x} \\ c_y \cdot s + \xi_{c,y} \end{pmatrix},
\quad \xi_{c,x}, \xi_{c,y} \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.13s, 0.13s).
\end{equation}
Here, $n_c$ governs target cell shape (e.g., $3$ for triangles, $4$ for quads),
$r_c=r(0.9+0.2\xi_c)$ with $\xi_c\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$ governs size, and
$J_c=\min(J,0.45\pi/n_c)$ limits angular irregularity. This cap preserves
circular vertex order and angular gaps below $\pi$ for $n_c\ge3$, giving
simple convex input polygons containing their centers. The assigned regions
need not be convex. Python and JavaScript share a Mulberry32 sequence and
draw order; the resulting realization differs from earlier prototypes.
The supported field is finite, with default $8\times12$ sites and $s=.9$. When unspecified, $n_c$ is sampled uniformly from $\{5,6,7,8\}$.
The shader accepts at most 256 cells with 3--12 vertices. Samples are pixel
centers over $[-s/2,(C-.5)s]\times[-s/2,(R-.5)s]$, with image $y$ downward.

\subsection{Signed Boundary Field}
\label{sec:signed}

Let $P_c$ denote the polygon of Eq.~\ref{eq:poly}. We define the signed boundary
distance field $s_c(\mathbf{x})$:
\begin{equation}
s_c(\mathbf{x}) =
\begin{cases}
-\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial P_c) & \mathbf{x}\in P_c\\
+\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial P_c) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:sdf}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial P_c) = \min_{\mathbf{y}\in\partial P_c} \lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\rVert_2$.

\paragraph{Mathematical Rationale for the Signed Field.}
Consider an energy term based on unsigned distance
$\beta \operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x}, \partial P_c)$. Because
$\operatorname{dist} \ge 0$ with $\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x}, \partial P_c) = 0$
precisely on the perimeter $\partial P_c$, the term attains its minimum along the
boundary. An unsigned boundary-distance term favors the perimeter rather than deeper interior points. The signed
term instead has negative values inside the polygon, attaining its most
negative value at a center of a largest inscribed disk. Its behavior is not
monotonic along every path toward the medial axis, and competition with the
other terms can still distort or remove a cell. Figure~\ref{fig:shape} shows
the separate effect of releasing coverage after assignment.

\subsection{The Three-Term Energy Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c$, $d_c = \lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert_2$,
and $\theta_c = \operatorname{atan2}(d_y, d_x)$. We assign each pixel
$\mathbf{x}$ to the cell minimizing:
\begin{equation}
E_c(\mathbf{x}) = \underbrace{\alpha d_c}_{\text{size}}
+ \underbrace{\beta\,s_c(\mathbf{x})}_{\text{shape}}
+ \underbrace{\gamma\bigl(1-\cos(\kappa\theta_c+\lambda d_c)\bigr)}_{\text{fracture}},
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
L(\mathbf{x}) = \arg\min_{c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}} E_c(\mathbf{x}).
\label{eq:argmin}
\end{equation}
Default weights are $\alpha=0.58$, $\beta=0.32$, $\gamma=0.10$, $\kappa=2$, and
$\lambda=1$. Setting $\beta = \gamma = 0$ collapses $E_c(\mathbf{x})$ to
$\alpha d_c$, recovering nearest-site assignment for $\alpha>0$.
Weights and $\lambda$ must be finite and nonnegative, with integer
$\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,6\}$. Ties select the smallest index; all-zero weights
select cell zero. For $d_c<10^{-6}$ the phase contribution is set to zero.
There is no direction-independent phase limit at the site; this is an explicit
convention, not continuity of $\operatorname{atan2}$.

The phase term $\gamma(1 - \cos(\kappa\theta_c + \lambda d_c))$ acts as an
angular standing wave propagating radially outward. Because neighbouring cells
have independent centres, their respective polar angle frames $\theta_i, \theta_j$
differ in contested border regions, causing cell boundaries to
deviate from straight bisectors into intricate, wandering crack paths.

\subsection{Coverage Margin and Space-Filling Decoupling}
\label{sec:margin}

Equation~\ref{eq:argmin} assigns every pixel, producing a space-filling partition of
the image domain. Under point-site Voronoi assignment with jittered positions, cell
boundaries are formed by perpendicular bisectors that generically intersect at 3-valent
junctions; as a result, cells cannot conform to prescribed target silhouettes (such as
equilateral triangles or rectangles) without distorting neighboring boundaries. Even
when introducing polygon distance into the energy, a strict space-filling partition
forces adjacent cells to share all intervening boundaries, producing compromise shapes
(the current default-phase triangle experiment gives $\mathrm{SF}=0.556$).

To restore shape control and allow cells to reflect target silhouettes, we decouple
cell assignment from the space-filling requirement by introducing a coverage margin
$m \in \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{equation}
L_m(\mathbf{x}) =
\begin{cases}
\varnothing & s_{L(\mathbf{x})}(\mathbf{x}) > m\\
L(\mathbf{x}) & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
Pixels whose signed distance to their assigned polygon exceeds $m$ are released as
background / grout ($\varnothing$). This accomplishes two critical objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
\item It frees cells from the space-filling constraint, permitting them to adhere
closely to their prescribed silhouettes ($m=0.0$ clips cells cleanly to their polygon
boundaries, giving mean triangular shape fidelity $0.945$ in Table~\ref{tbl:shape}).
\item It provides a monotonic coverage threshold:
$m > 0$ permits controlled cell dilation, $m = 0$ clips cells exactly to their
polygons, and $m < 0$ erodes them. Coverage is monotonic for fixed labels,
but physical gap width is not guaranteed constant; released pixels are not
reassigned to another cell.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Directability and Control Quantitative Studies}
\label{sec:control}

\paragraph{Metric.} We evaluate shape fidelity ($\mathrm{SF}$) as the mean
Intersection over Union (IoU) between each rendered cell and its generating
polygon:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SF} = \frac{1}{|C_{\text{valid}}|}\sum_{c \in C_{\text{valid}}}
\frac{|\{\mathbf{x} : L_m(\mathbf{x}) = c\} \cap P_c|}{|\{\mathbf{x} : L_m(\mathbf{x}) = c\} \cup P_c|}.
\end{equation}
Eligibility uses the target polygon intersected with the image domain,
not the output area: targets with at least 16 pixels are included, and
vanished output cells score zero. We average cells within each seed and then
eight seeds (7, 22, 42, 100--104). Unless stated otherwise, this study uses
$320\times200$, 96 sites, $r=.62$, $J=.05$, analytical distance and default
energy weights. Per-seed values, standard deviations and coverage are saved.

\section{Raster Geometry and Weighted-Diagram Comparisons}
\label{sec:reach}

\paragraph{Continuous Properties and Raster Evidence.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Power Diagrams}: In a power diagram, cell boundaries are lines
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}\cdot(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_j)
=\tfrac12\bigl(&\lVert\mathbf{u}_i\rVert^2-\lVert\mathbf{u}_j\rVert^2\\
&-w_i+w_j\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Cells are intersections of half-planes, which are mathematically guaranteed to be
\emph{convex} in the continuous plane $\mathbb{R}^2$~\cite{aurenhammer1987power}.
\item \textbf{Apollonius Diagrams}: In additively weighted diagrams, bisectors
are branches of hyperbolas. While cells can be non-convex, nonempty cells are
mathematically guaranteed to be \emph{connected}. A continuous partition containing
a disconnected cell (multiple disjoint spatial components) cannot be produced by any
Apollonius diagram.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Experimental Benchmark.} We evaluate solidity $S=A_{\rm pix}/A_{\rm hull}$, the ratio of pixel area to convex-hull area, and components on $320\times200$
raster labels, seed 22, without clipping. Hulls use all four corners of each
pixel. Components use 8-connectivity and minimum area $\lceil10^{-4}WH\rceil$
(7 pixels here). Cells below this area are excluded. The solidity threshold
$S<.98$ is descriptive. Baselines share sites; power weights are $r_c^2$ and
Apollonius weights are $r_c$. Analytical distance is used for MORONI.

\section{Conservative Acceleration and Error Analysis}
\label{sec:fast}

\subsection{An Admissible Incumbent-Based Energy Bound}

Evaluating brute-force energy over $N$ cells for $P$ pixels requires $O(NP)$
work. Each generating polygon is contained in its radius-$r_c$ disk. Thus
$s_c(\mathbf x)\ge d_c-r_c$: outside the polygon this follows from triangle
inequality, and inside, distance to its boundary cannot exceed distance to
the enclosing disk boundary. Nonnegative weights and phase yield
\begin{equation}
E_c(\mathbf x)\ge(\alpha+\beta)d_c-\beta r_c.
\label{eq:lower_bound}
\end{equation}
For hybrid distance, use $r_c+\varepsilon_c$, where $\varepsilon_c$ bounds
lookup error (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_lut}). Given incumbent $U$, reject
only when
\begin{equation}
(\alpha+\beta)d_c-\beta(r_c+\varepsilon_c)>U.
\label{eq:incumbent_prune}
\end{equation}
Analytical mode uses $\varepsilon_c=0$. Equality remains eligible for index
ties. The real-arithmetic bound does not by itself prove floating-point
correctness; the CPU uses a relative slack of $10^{-12}$ and separately tests
observed agreement. No sampled polygon in-radius is treated as an exact bound.

\subsection{Lattice Binning and Ring Escalation}

Cells reside on a jittered lattice of spacing $s$. The renderer visits
Chebyshev rings around the nearest valid lattice anchor. Unvisited sites lie
in up to four strips. Distance to half-planes containing these strips,
expanded by $0.13s$ site jitter, lower-bounds the remaining site distance $D$.
For example, the first unvisited right column $j$ has $x_c\ge(j-.13)s$ and
contributes $\max(0,(j-.13)s-x)$. Absent strips contribute $+\infty$; take
the minimum over present strips. A query terminates only when
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{floor}}=(\alpha+\beta)D-\beta\max_c(r_c+\varepsilon_c)>U.
\label{eq:ring_floor}
\end{equation}
Otherwise rings continue until the finite field is exhausted. This also works
for exterior queries and all-zero weights, without division by $\alpha$ or
a fixed two-ring cutoff. Bins and bounds are recomputed when query positions
or parameters change. The worst case remains $O(NP)$; no majority-at-ring-zero
or constant-cost guarantee is made.

\subsection{Phase Evaluation}

The corrected implementation uses the direct expression in Eq.~\ref{eq:energy} for all
supported harmonics 1--6; no phase LUT or trigonometric-elimination speedup
is claimed. For $d_c<10^{-6}$, the phase contribution is explicitly zero,
since the angular term has no direction-independent limit at the center.

\subsection{Discretization Error of the Signed Field LUT}

$[-B_c,B_c]^2$ with $B_c=1.5r_c$. Queries inside use bilinear interpolation;
queries outside use analytical polygon distance. A clamped lookup plus
distance to the box is not an exact SDF extension. We test 4,000 uniform

\label{alg:main}

\State \Return $L$
\end{algorithmic}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limits}

The current field is finite, planar and nonperiodic. Periodic tiling,
curved-surface evaluation, mobile performance and cross-vendor numerical
behavior require additional implementation and testing. Four preview samples
do not solve minification or temporal aliasing; baked maps require suitable
downstream filtering. The controls interact, and stronger phase can reduce
target fidelity. Small or nearly tied cells are sensitive to precision and
sampling. The square-root height has steep gradients near zero, and the site
phase convention is discontinuous. The CPU fallback uses raster differences
for normals and is not covered by GPU timing/diagnostic claims. Material names
indicate visual inspiration; physical fracture and artist preference have not
been validated. An artist study remains future work, not an established result.

\section{Conclusion}

MORONI combines polygon targets, signed-distance assignment, angular
phase and coverage release as a practical cellular texture recipe. Matched
experiments show that clipping contributes most of the silhouette fidelity;
polygon-aware arbitration adds a smaller benefit, while phase can trade
fidelity for variation. Conservative disk bounds and adaptive finite search
support acceleration, with approximation error and numerical tests reported
separately. Shared geometry, measured GPU execution and explicit export
semantics connect the browser studio to the CPU artifact. The limitations
remain part of the technique's intended scope.

\section{Full Procedural Generator Algorithms}
\label{sec:appendix_generators}

To highlight the mathematical foundations, practical benefits, and drawbacks
of existing cellular noise functions, we provide complete algorithmic
formulations for Standard Voronoi, Power diagrams, Additively Weighted
Apollonius diagrams, Worley noise, and MORONI.

\subsection{Standard Voronoi Generator (\texorpdfstring{$L_2$}{L2} Euclidean)}

Standard Voronoi partitions $\mathbb{R}^2$ into convex polyhedral cells based
on Euclidean distance to point sites $\mathbf{u}_c$:
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{Vor}}(\mathbf{x}) = \arg\min_{c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}} \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Standard Voronoi Generator}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query point $\mathbf{x}$, sites $\{\mathbf{u}_c\}_{c=0}^{N-1}$
\State $d^\star \gets \infty$;\; $c^\star \gets 0$
\ForAll{$c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$}
  \State $d \gets \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2$
  \If{$d < d^\star$}
    \State $d^\star \gets d$;\; $c^\star \gets c$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return cell index $c^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Practical Benefits.} Nearest-site queries admit established
spatial data structures; cells partition the domain and have straight edges.
\paragraph{Drawbacks.} Site positions indirectly affect shape. Square cells
are possible, but arbitrary independent polygon targets are not generally
specified by sites alone. Separate clipping or distance thresholds can add gaps.

\subsection{Power (Laguerre) Diagram Generator}

Power diagrams assign scalar weights $w_c \in \mathbb{R}$ to each site, defining
the power distance~\cite{aurenhammer1987power}:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{pow}(\mathbf{x}, c) = \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2^2 - w_c,
\qquad
L_{\text{Power}}(\mathbf{x}) = \arg\min_c \operatorname{pow}(\mathbf{x}, c).
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Power (Laguerre) Diagram Generator}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query point $\mathbf{x}$, weighted sites $\{(\mathbf{u}_c, w_c)\}_{c=0}^{N-1}$
\State $p^\star \gets \infty$;\; $c^\star \gets 0$
\ForAll{$c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$}
  \State $p \gets \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2^2 - w_c$
  \If{$p < p^\star$}
    \State $p^\star \gets p$;\; $c^\star \gets c$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return cell index $c^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Practical Benefits.} Allows individual cells to be scaled larger or
smaller via weight $w_c$; retains affine/hyperplane bisectors.
\paragraph{Drawbacks.} Cells are strict intersections of half-planes and are
mathematically guaranteed to be convex in the continuous plane; sites with small
weights can be entirely eclipsed (empty cells); cannot produce concavity or
fracture.

\subsection{Additively Weighted (Apollonius) Diagram}

Apollonius diagrams subtract weights directly from the Euclidean distance:
\begin{equation}
d_{\text{Apol}}(\mathbf{x}, c) = \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2 - w_c,
\qquad
L_{\text{Apol}}(\mathbf{x}) = \arg\min_c d_{\text{Apol}}(\mathbf{x}, c).
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Apollonius Diagram Generator}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query point $\mathbf{x}$, weighted sites $\{(\mathbf{u}_c, w_c)\}_{c=0}^{N-1}$
\State $d^\star \gets \infty$;\; $c^\star \gets 0$
\ForAll{$c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$}
  \State $d \gets \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2 - w_c$
  \If{$d < d^\star$}
    \State $d^\star \gets d$;\; $c^\star \gets c$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return cell index $c^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Practical Benefits.} Boundaries are hyperbolas, admitting curved
cell boundaries and possible nonconvexity.
\paragraph{Drawbacks.} Nonempty cells are connected in the continuous plane;
site positions and additive weights jointly govern boundaries, without explicit
polygon targets.

\subsection{Worley Cellular Texture Basis}

Worley noise~\cite{worley1996} computes the distances $F_1 \le F_2 \le \dots$ to
ordered nearest feature points:
\begin{equation}
F_1(\mathbf{x}) = \min_c \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert,
\quad
F_2(\mathbf{x}) = \min_{c \ne c^\star} \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert.
\end{equation}
The classic cellular texture is obtained via $F_2(\mathbf{x}) - F_1(\mathbf{x})$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Worley Cellular Basis ($F_1, F_2$)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query point $\mathbf{x}$, sites $\{\mathbf{u}_c\}_{c=0}^{N-1}$
\State $F_1 \gets \infty$;\; $F_2 \gets \infty$
\ForAll{$c \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$}
  \State $d \gets \lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c \rVert_2$
  \If{$d < F_1$}
    \State $F_2 \gets F_1$;\; $F_1 \gets d$
  \ElsIf{$d < F_2$}
    \State $F_2 \gets d$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $(F_1, F_2, F_2 - F_1)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Practical Benefits.} Produces continuous intensity gradients useful
for stone outlines and caustic bubbles.
\paragraph{Drawbacks.} Underlying cell partition is identical to Voronoi;
thresholds on $F_2-F_1$ can form seams, but their physical width depends
on the configuration and chosen threshold.

\subsection{MORONI Generator}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Full MORONI Generator with Grout Release}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query point $\mathbf{x}$, cells $\{(\mathbf{u}_c, P_c, \mathrm{LUT}_c)\}$,
weights $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$, frequencies $\kappa,\lambda$, margin $m$
\State $E^\star \gets \infty$;\; $c^\star \gets 0$;\; $s^\star \gets 0$
\ForAll{cells $c$ in increasing index order}
  \State $\mathbf{d} \gets \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u}_c$;\; $d_c \gets \lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert_2$
  \State $s_c\gets$ analytical distance, or hybrid lookup with exact exterior
  \State $p_c\gets 0$
  \If{$d_c\ge10^{-6}$}
    \State $\theta_c\gets\operatorname{atan2}(d_y,d_x)$
    \State $p_c\gets1-\cos(\kappa\theta_c+\lambda d_c)$
  \EndIf
  \State $E \gets \alpha d_c + \beta s_c + \gamma p_c$
  \If{$E < E^\star$}
    \State $E^\star \gets E$;\; $c^\star \gets c$;\; $s^\star \gets s_c$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$s^\star > m$}
  \State \Return $\varnothing$ \Comment{Released as grout/mortar background}
\Else
  \State \Return $c^\star$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Analytical Error Bound for Bilinear LUT Sampling}
\label{sec:appendix_lut}

Let $s_c(\mathbf{x})$ be the exact signed distance and $\hat s_c$ its bilinear approximation.
The lookup half-width is $B_c=1.5r_c$ and grid spacing $h_c=2B_c/(L-1)$.
For exact corner samples, signed distance is 1-Lipschitz. At offset $(a,b)$
within a square, the bilinear value is a convex combination of its corner
values; the error is bounded by their weighted mean distance to the query.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
|s_c-\hat s_c|\le\sqrt{a(h_c-a)+b(h_c-b)}\le h_c/\sqrt2=\varepsilon_c.
\end{equation}
Outside the box the implementation evaluates analytical distance, giving
zero approximation error there. Floating-point roundoff is additional.
The bilinear reconstruction need not itself be 1-Lipschitz, and switching
to analytical evaluation can create a small box-boundary discontinuity.
The largest bound among default polygons is 0.01639 for $L=72$, compared
with maximum observed error 0.00847 in Table~\ref{tbl:lut_err}.

With $\varepsilon=\max_c\varepsilon_c$, an analytical winning label whose
lead over every competitor exceeds $2\beta\varepsilon$ is preserved under
the hybrid approximation. A smaller lead need not change the label. This
condition alone supplies no universal area or mismatch fraction without
further regularity assumptions about competing energy fields.

\section{GPU Fragment Shader Pipeline}
\label{sec:appendix_shader}

The WebGL fragment shader computes the three-term energy from an uploaded float32 geometry
texture, traversing all finite candidates with disk-bound rejection. Polygon
distance uses segment projection and a ray-crossing sign test. Phase uses
the direct expression, with the same explicit center convention as the CPU.
The shader supports analytical distance; it does not use the CPU spatial LUT.

For retained pixels define $h=\sqrt{\operatorname{clamp}(-s_c/r,0,1)}$ and
zero for background. Normal differences use world offset $\epsilon=.005s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf n=\operatorname{normalize}(-.06s\,h_x,-.06s\,h_y,1).
\end{equation}
RGB encodes $(\mathbf n+1)/2$; positive image $y$ points down. Consumers
using the opposite tangent convention must invert green. Roughness is the
illustrative constant .25 in cells and .95 in gaps. Data maps are linear
data; albedo is an unlit display palette with no baked surface lighting.
Exports render one sample per pixel and exclude wireframe overlays. The
SVG command exports generating polygons only. These map choices are not
recovered physical material properties.

\subsection{Laboratory Architecture and Live Modes}

The laboratory operates entirely client-side with zero dependencies or server
requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Interactive Dials Sidebar}: Sliders update the field and WebGL uniforms.
End-to-end interaction latency is distinct from the measured GPU draw time.
\item \textbf{Top Viewport Mode Bar}: Allows instantaneous toggling between:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Paper Colors}: Unlit cell identity colors shared with the Python
  figures. Shaded mode and edge darkening are removed.
  \item \textbf{Tangent-Space Normal Map}: Dynamic 3D surface normals computed
  via central finite differences.
  \item \textbf{Height / Displacement Map}: Grayscale elevation map mapping
  interior signed distance to dome curvature.
  \item \textbf{Roughness / Grout Mask}: Two-valued mask distinguishing
  smooth cell tiles from rough recessed mortar.
  \item \textbf{Energy Field Decomposition}: Direct visual inspection of the
  isolated components: centre distance $d_c$, signed polygon field $s_c(\mathbf{x})$,
  and angular phase interference wave $(1-\cos)$.
  \item \textbf{A/B Split-Screen Comparison}: Allows dragging an interactive
  divider directly across the screen to contrast standard Voronoi against MORONI.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Live Geometric \& Topological HUD}: A $180\times120$ GPU label
readback drives raster solidity and retained 8-connected component counts.
The minimum component area is $10^{-4}$ of the sampled domain. These are
descriptive samples, not a proof of diagram-family membership or continuous
topology. GPU timing is marked unavailable where timer queries are unsupported.
\end{enumerate}
